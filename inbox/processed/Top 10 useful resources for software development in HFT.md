These are 10 resources I’d find most useful starting as a developer, quant developer, or a quant researcher who has to code extensively, in HFT today.

3. **Agner Fog’s research on computer architecture, compilers, and software optimization.** I’ve referred to this many times for optimization. ([Link](https://www.agner.org/))
4. **Compiler Explorer.** Quite useful for debugging. Matt works at Aquatic, a quant trading firm with good pedigree. ([link](https://godbolt.org/))
5. **C++ Core Guidelines.** Best practices in C++. ([link](https://github.com/isocpp/CppCoreGuidelines))
6. **Databento.** From time to time I’ve just needed some order book data, top of book data, or security definitions quick and easy in normalized format and over web. ([link](https://docs.databento.com/))
7. **ef\_vi and Xilinx manuals.** Have had to refer to this many times. ([link](https://github.com/majek/openonload/blob/master/README.ef_vi))
8. **Erik Rigtorp’s blog.** Erik has kindly maintained a high quality set of blog posts that constantly show up, and has a good open source hash map implementation for reference. ([link](https://rigtorp.se/))
9. **Folly SPSC queue design.** The most common structure in HFT after an array/vector is probably a queue. ([link](https://github.com/facebook/folly/blob/main/folly/ProducerConsumerQueue.h))
10. **LMAX Disruptor, SBE and Mechanical Sympathy.** Martin Thompson has given good talks on cache optimization, false sharing, etc. and made great contributions to developer education in the field. ([link](https://lmax-exchange.github.io/disruptor/))
11. **McKay Brothers network map.** I find myself referring to this very often. ([link](https://www.mckay-brothers.com/product-page/#Networkmap))
12. **OnixS FIX reference dictionary.** OnixS have kindly kept this easy-to-read FIX dictionary available to public on their site. ([link](https://www.onixs.biz/fix-dictionary.html))
